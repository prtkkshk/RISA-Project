Why these rates are conservative: last year's warm channels won 24 of 44 brands touched (55%). The base case assumes 45 to 50%, the stretch case 50 to 55%. Leaked brands get half of that. Warm deals took a median of 16 days from first touch to confirmed, so a 10-week campaign fits 3 or more cycles.

Who we target, and why they say yes

  • Prioritise: food and beverage, mobility, consumer D2C (16 of 24 wins, ₹14.9L). Motive: trial and sampling with 18 to 24 year olds, app installs, campus hiring brand.
  • Selection criteria: (1) a warm path exists, (2) a Partnerships Manager owns the budget, (3) the brand can pay cash, (4) the category isn't already sold.

Constraints

  • Team capacity: 3 owners handled 58 deals last year while spending 82% of touches on cold channels that closed nothing. The stretch case needs the same deal count, all warm.
  • Cash timing: invoice at signing, 50% advance, everything due by festival day minus 7.

What moves the number

±20% on intro volume, win rate, deal size or collection gives ₹19.6L to ₹29.8L. Intro volume is the driver we control, so it's bet #1.

  • Scale cold email: 507 touches across 91 brands produced 0 partnerships and 7 opt-outs. It costs owner-hours and brand reputation.
  • Cold LinkedIn sequences: 12 deals and 0 lasting wins. The only confirmation withdrew.
  • Count in-kind value toward the target: it isn't cash. We'll accept in-kind only on top of a cash floor.

Dependencies and overlap

  • Bet 1 feeds bet 4: packages need warm pipeline to test on.
  • Bet 2 is a multiplier on every other bet, so it starts on day 1.
  • Bets 1 and 5 share Riya. If bet 5 shows a stop signal, her time goes back to bet 1.

Biggest risks

  • Partners won't refer. Mitigation: make it part of the contract, with bonus inventory.
  • Warm win rate drops as volume grows. The model already discounts it from 55% to 45%.
  • Advance terms slow down closing. Fallback: 30% advance.

Weekly checks across all bets

  • Cash gap: confirmed minus collected. Every confirmed deal not yet fully paid raises an alert.
  • Pipeline age: a deal with no stage change for 14 days gets auto-flagged, then re-routed or closed.
  • Leakage: any qualified brand without a deal is flagged until someone opens one.
  • Forecast vs target: uses each channel's own closed win rate, so dead cold pipeline counts as ₹0.

How the dashboard drives the decision

  • Thresholds live inside each initiative. A new KPI reading recomputes scale / change / stop automatically.
  • "What needs attention" turns raw data into ranked alerts: the target gap, 0-win channels, stale deals, uncollected cash, withdrawals.
  • The snapshot date picker replays any past date with no hindsight, which tests whether a signal would really have been visible at the time.

How the AI analyst helps (and where it stops)

  • Answers only from live data through 17 tools, cites them, and labels evidence, simulated data and assumptions.
  • Diagnoses the "why". For example, it shows that owner gaps disappear once you adjust for channel mix.
  • Proposes tasks, stage changes and payments. A human confirms each one, and every action is audit-logged.
  • Says when the data can't answer: loss reasons, cost per sponsor, causal uplift.
